# 05 · "Psionics": A Critical Assessment

The project brief asks about "psionics." This file gives the term its history, reviews the strongest evidence ever assembled for psi phenomena, and states the evidence-based conclusion. Per the source-triangulation rule, government primary documents are used alongside mainstream reviews, and fringe interpretations are tagged.

---

## 5.1 What the word actually is

**"Psionics"** = *psi* + *electronics*, popularized in the 1950s by **John W. Campbell**, editor of *Astounding Science Fiction*, who promoted devices like the "Hieronymus machine" (T. Galen Hieronymus' 1949 US patent 2,482,773 `[PRIMARY]` — a patent demonstrates filing, not function). The word was born in **science fiction and fringe engineering**, and today survives mainly in fantasy gaming (D&D psionics) and New Age materials. The academic field studying purported psychic phenomena calls itself **parapsychology**.

This matters for the project: a research program cannot "use psionics" as a method, because psionics is not an established phenomenon or instrument. What it *can* do is study (a) the history of the claim and (b) why resonant sacred spaces generate experiences that people *report* in psychic terms — which is a real, tractable psychology question (see 04, 07).

## 5.2 The strongest evidence ever assembled — and how it held up

### Laboratory parapsychology
- **J.B. Rhine (Duke, 1930s):** card-guessing ESP experiments; founded the field. Early positive results degraded under tightened controls; critics documented sensory-leakage and statistical problems. `[MAINSTREAM]` critique; Rhine's *Extra-Sensory Perception* (1934) `[PRIMARY/FRINGE]`.
- **Ganzfeld telepathy** (sensory-deprivation receiver guesses a sender's target): the field's best-regarded paradigm. **Bem & Honorton (1994, *Psychological Bulletin*)** reported ~32% hit rate vs. 25% chance; **Milton & Wiseman (1999)** meta-analyzed subsequent replications: effect near zero. Later pro-psi meta-analyses (Storm et al. 2010) and skeptical rebuttals continue; the effect appears and disappears depending on inclusion criteria — the signature of a literature built on flexible analysis rather than a real phenomenon. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Bem (2011) "Feeling the Future"** (*JPSP*): precognition claims that passed peer review — then failed high-powered registered replication (Galak et al. 2012; Ritchie, Wiseman & French 2012) and became a canonical trigger of psychology's **replication crisis**. The episode is now studied as a methods cautionary tale, not as evidence for psi. `[MAINSTREAM]`

### Government remote-viewing programs
- **CIA/DIA "Stargate" program (1972–1995):** ~$20M over two decades on remote viewing (SRI work by Puthoff & Targ; viewers incl. Ingo Swann, Pat Price, Joseph McMoneagle). Declassified files are public at the CIA reading room `[PRIMARY]`.
- **Termination review (1995, American Institutes for Research):** statistician **Jessica Utts** concluded a statistically anomalous effect existed in some lab data; psychologist **Ray Hyman** concluded the anomalies were unexplained but methodologically uninterpretable and unreplicated by independent labs. Both agreed on the operational bottom line, and the CIA acted on it: **remote viewing never produced actionable intelligence, and the program was closed.** `[PRIMARY]` (AIR report) + `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Divergence note:** fringe sources cite Stargate's *existence* and Utts' statistical note as government proof of psi, and omit the Hyman assessment, the zero-operational-value finding, and the termination. The primary documents contain all of it; reading them whole reverses the fringe conclusion. This is the clearest case in the project of credibility-laundering-by-partial-citation.

## 5.3 Evidence-based conclusion

- **No psi effect has ever survived** the standard trio: preregistration, adequate power, independent replication.
- The mainstream scientific position (e.g., Reber & Alcock 2019, *American Psychologist*) is that the prior probability is extremely low and the evidential record is best explained by methodological artifact. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Project stance (H4):** psionics claims are treated as *cultural data* — beliefs to be explained — not as mechanisms. If a future preregistered, multi-lab, adversarially-collaborated result emerges, the stance updates; the falsification criteria are stated in 07 §7.6.

## 5.4 Why sacred acoustics *generates* psychic reports (the productive reframe)

The genuinely interesting link between "psionics" and this project is that **resonant sacred environments reliably produce the raw experiences that psychic frameworks were invented to explain**:

| Reported experience | Documented ordinary mechanism |
|---|---|
| "A presence in the chamber" | Infrasound (04 §4.7); unlocatable reverberant sound (02 §2.6); darkness + refuge-enclosure |
| "The voice wasn't mine / came from everywhere" | Room-mode coupling with chant: the room re-radiates the singer's voice from all surfaces |
| "Time distortion, unity, small self" | Awe response (04 §4.3); absorption in drone/chant |
| "Energy in my body" | Vibrotactile stimulation near chamber resonance at chant loudness — chest/skull vibration is physically real |
| "Visions in the dark" | Sensory-deprivation hallucination (ganzfeld conditions occur naturally in a lightless tomb) |

Note the irony in the last row: **the ganzfeld — parapsychology's flagship method — is essentially a lab reconstruction of a Neolithic chamber**: sensory deprivation, relaxation, ambiguous input, and an expectation frame. Both settings reliably produce vivid imagery; only the interpretation differs. That symmetry, not any psychic mechanism, is the finding.
