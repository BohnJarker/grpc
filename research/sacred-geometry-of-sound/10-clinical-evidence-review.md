# 10 · Sound-Based Interventions: What the Clinical Literature Actually Shows

The modern "sound healing" industry is the commercial descendant of the sacred-sound traditions in 03. This file reviews the published evidence, ordered from best-evidenced to least. It matters for the project because fringe frequency claims (06) routinely borrow credibility from the *well*-evidenced end of this spectrum.

> Written from the standard literature of these fields; per the project rule (08), each citation is to be page-checked before formal publication.

---

## 10.1 Music therapy — the well-evidenced end `[MAINSTREAM]`

Cochrane systematic reviews (the strictest evidence bar in medicine) find **moderate-quality evidence** that music-based interventions reduce anxiety (notably pre-operative and cancer-care settings, e.g., the Bradt et al. reviews), improve depressive symptoms as an adjunct to standard care, and modestly help behavioral/emotional symptoms in dementia. Effects are real, useful, and *non-specific to any frequency*: they ride on preference, memory, attention, breathing, and the therapeutic relationship. **No Cochrane review attributes benefit to particular Hertz values.**

**Bottom line:** sound demonstrably affects mood and physiology in clinically useful ways — through psychology, not through frequency magic. This is the strongest legitimate support for this project's H2.

## 10.2 Singing bowls / sound baths `[MAINSTREAM, weak]`

- Goldsby et al. (2017, *Journal of Evidence-Based Complementary & Alternative Medicine*): observational pre/post study (~60 participants) of a sound-bath session — reduced tension, anger, fatigue, with the largest gains in first-timers; **no control group, no blinding**.
- Small subsequent trials and reviews: consistent short-term relaxation effects, comparable to other relaxation practices (guided rest, meditation); nothing distinguishing the bowls' spectra from generic calming stimulation.
- Combine with the provenance problem (03 §3.2: "Tibetan" singing-bowl meditation is largely a modern Western invention) and the honest reading is: **sound baths are a pleasant, probably effective relaxation ritual whose mechanism is rest + expectation + absorption**, not ancient frequency technology.

## 10.3 Vibroacoustic therapy (felt low-frequency vibration, ~20–120 Hz) `[MAINSTREAM, preliminary]`

Originated with **Olav Skille** (Norway, 1980s); applies audio-range vibration through beds/chairs. Small trials report reduced muscle tension, spasticity, and pain-related distress; reviews consistently conclude *promising but underpowered, poorly blinded, heterogeneous*. Interesting for this project because it is the clinical cousin of the chamber-resonance vibrotactile mechanism (09 §9.5): the one modality here with a physically plausible **somatic** pathway — and note its working band overlaps the 60–130 Hz chamber-mode band of 09 §9.1.

## 10.4 Binaural beats `[MAINSTREAM meta-analysis, mixed]`

Two tones slightly detuned between the ears produce a perceived beat; marketed as "brainwave entrainment." The Garcia-Argibay et al. meta-analysis (2019, *Psychological Research*) finds small-to-moderate aggregate effects on anxiety, attention, and memory across heterogeneous, mostly small studies — but effects do **not** cleanly track the advertised frequency-band logic, and EEG evidence of genuine cortical entrainment is inconsistent. Bottom line: possibly a mild relaxation/masking aid; the brainwave-tuning marketing narrative is unsupported by the EEG record.

## 10.5 40 Hz gamma sensory stimulation (GENUS) `[MAINSTREAM, active frontier]`

The most scientifically serious "specific frequency" research program in existence: MIT's Tsai lab (Iaccarino et al. 2016, *Nature*; Martorell et al. 2019, *Cell*) showed 40 Hz light/sound stimulation reduces amyloid pathology and improves cognition in mouse models of Alzheimer's; small human trials (Chan et al., *PLOS ONE* 2022 and follow-ups; Cognito Therapeutics device trials) report safety, gamma entrainment on EEG, and preliminary slowing of brain atrophy — with larger trials ongoing. Two lessons for this project: (1) a genuine frequency-specific neural effect looks like *this* — mechanism, dose-response, preregistered trials, independent replication attempts — a useful contrast template against which 432/528 Hz claims visibly fail; (2) even here, benefits remain unproven in humans until the large trials read out — the project cites GENUS as *frontier*, not fact.

## 10.6 Om chanting and group chant `[MAINSTREAM, small studies]`

Kalyani et al. (2011, *International Journal of Yoga*): fMRI during Om chanting vs. "ssss" vocalization control (n≈12) — limbic deactivation pattern resembling vagus-nerve stimulation; the authors proposed the humming/vibration → vagal afferent pathway. Small, unreplicated at scale, but mechanistically plausible: humming is known to alter nasal nitric-oxide release and slow exhalation, and slow chant paces breathing near the ~6 breaths/min band that maximizes heart-rate variability — a well-documented relaxation physiology. Group chant/choir studies additionally show heart-rate synchronization across singers (Vickhoff et al. 2013, *Frontiers in Psychology*).

**Bottom line:** chanting has real, measurable calming physiology — driven by breath pacing, vibration, and social synchrony, all pitch-independent. The ancient practice works; the modern Hertz-numerology explanation of *why* it works is unnecessary and unsupported.

## 10.7 Synthesis: the evidence gradient

| Intervention | Evidence strength | Frequency-specific? |
|---|---|---|
| Music therapy | Strong (Cochrane) | No |
| Chant/breath practices | Moderate (mechanistic + small trials) | No — breath and vibration, not pitch |
| Sound baths | Weak (uncontrolled) | No |
| Vibroacoustic therapy | Preliminary | Band-specific (low Hz, somatic) — plausibly |
| Binaural beats | Mixed meta-analysis | Advertised logic unsupported |
| 40 Hz GENUS | Frontier — trials ongoing | Yes — the only rigorously tested candidate |
| 432/528 Hz "healing" | None beyond expectation (06 §§1–2) | Claimed, unsupported |

The gradient tells one coherent story: **sound heals through psychology and physiology (attention, memory, breath, vibration, expectation, social bonding) — and the more precisely a claim locates the benefit in a magic number of Hertz, the weaker its evidence gets.** The single possible exception (40 Hz gamma) earned that status through exactly the methods (07) this project prescribes.
