# 04 · The Psychology of Geometric Space

Answering RQ3: what controlled research shows about how the **shape** of a room affects the mind — and how geometry and acoustics are two faces of the same variable (geometry *determines* a room's acoustic signature).

---

## 4.1 Curved vs. angular

- **Vartanian et al. (2013, *PNAS*):** participants judged curvilinear interiors more beautiful than rectilinear ones; fMRI showed curvilinear spaces preferentially engaged the anterior cingulate cortex (reward/emotion). `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Bar & Neta (2006, *Psychological Science*):** sharp angular objects elicit more amygdala activation than curved equivalents — an implicit threat interpretation of sharp contours. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- Relevance: domes, apses, passage-tomb corbelling, kivas — sacred architecture is disproportionately **curved**, and curvature is both psychologically preferred and acoustically focusing (a dome literally concentrates reflected sound and chant).

## 4.2 Ceiling height and the "cathedral effect"

- **Meyers-Levy & Zhu (2007, *Journal of Consumer Research*):** high ceilings prime abstraction/freedom-oriented thinking; low ceilings prime confinement and detail-focus. `[MAINSTREAM]` (effect sizes modest; consumer-context studies — extrapolate cautiously).
- Sacred architecture exploits **both poles**: the crawl-passage → vast chamber sequence (Newgrange, Hypogeum, Gothic west-door → nave) is a compression-release script: constrict the body and the visual field, then explode the volume. The emotional payoff of the release is proportional to the compression.

## 4.3 Awe — the load-bearing emotion

- **Keltner & Haidt (2003):** awe = perceived vastness + need for accommodation (mental schema revision). `[MAINSTREAM]` — the founding framework of awe research.
- Downstream findings (Piff et al. 2015; Shiota et al.; Bai et al. 2017): awe produces the **"small self"** — reduced self-focus, increased prosociality, altered time perception, increased comfort with uncertainty. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **This is the honest mechanism behind "sacred" space:** monumental interior volume is a reliable awe generator, and awe measurably makes people feel small, connected, and open to belief revision — exactly the psychological state religious ritual seeks. Joye & Verpooten (2013) argue monumental religious architecture evolved culturally *because* it exploits this response. `[MAINSTREAM]`

## 4.4 Enclosure, prospect, and refuge

- **Appleton's prospect-refuge theory (1975)** `[MAINSTREAM, classic]`: humans prefer spaces offering outlook + protection. Chambered sacred spaces are extreme-refuge environments: total enclosure, darkness, one guarded entrance — which heightens vulnerability, intimacy, and suggestibility. Sensory-deprivation research (and its modern flotation-REST descendant) shows dark silent enclosure alone can induce altered states and hallucination `[MAINSTREAM]` — no sound required; sound then shapes the state.

## 4.5 Symmetry, proportion, and the golden-ratio question

- Preference for bilateral symmetry in facades/spaces: robust. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Golden ratio (φ) aesthetic superiority: not supported.** George Markowsky (1992, *College Mathematics Journal*, "Misconceptions about the Golden Ratio") dismantled the empirical and historical case (the Parthenon/Great Pyramid φ claims rest on cherry-picked measurement choices); controlled preference studies find no reliable peak at φ. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Divergence note:** φ-mysticism is the geometric twin of 432 Hz-mysticism — a real, historically interesting ratio inflated into a universal law by selective measurement. Genuine documented proportion systems in sacred architecture (ad quadratum/ad triangulum in Gothic design, Vitruvius' modular system, mandala-based Vastu plans `[PRIMARY]`) used **simple ratios and geometric construction**, which is documented sacred geometry — no φ-doctrine needed.

## 4.6 Where geometry becomes acoustics (the project's central loop)

Room shape *is* an acoustic filter. The psychological effects of §4.1–4.4 arrive bundled with the acoustic consequences of the same geometry:

| Geometric choice | Visual/psychological effect | Simultaneous acoustic effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dome/vault | Preference (curvature), awe (height) | Focusing, long reverberation |
| Small stone chamber | Extreme refuge, sensory deprivation | Strong low-frequency modal resonance (~100 Hz for meter-scale rooms) |
| Long passage | Compression, procession | Waveguide; filters and delays sound from chamber |
| Vast nave | Awe (vastness) | 4–10 s reverberation → envelopment |
| Stone circle | Boundary, center-focus | Reflection back into the ring (Watson & Keating; Cox) |

**Implication for H2:** a visitor to Newgrange or Hagia Sophia receives a *multisensory package* — refuge-darkness + awe-volume + curvature preference + resonant envelopment + (in ritual) chant, incense, procession. Attributing the resulting numinous feeling to any single frequency is a category error; the room is a full-stack experience machine.

## 4.7 Infrasound and "presence" — the eerie edge

- **Vic Tandy (1998, *JSPR*, "The Ghost in the Machine"):** traced a "haunted" lab's apparitions and dread to a 19 Hz standing wave from a fan — near eyeball resonance (~18 Hz, per aerospace vibration research). Anecdotal n=1, but a famous, plausible mechanism report. `[MAINSTREAM-adjacent; published in a parapsychology journal but skeptical in content]`
- **Wiseman et al. (2003, mass concert experiment):** covertly adding ~17 Hz infrasound to live music increased reports of chills, unease, and odd sensations. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- Relevance: large drums, long horns, wind over megalithic structures, and big organ pipes (32-ft pipes ≈ 16 Hz) all generate infrasound; churches with such organ stops are, deliberately or not, running the Wiseman experiment on every congregation.
- **Divergence note:** fringe sources cite Tandy/Wiseman as proof that ancients "used infrasound technology." The evidence supports only: infrasound *can* cause eerie sensations, and *some* ritual sound sources produce infrasound. Systematic infrasound measurement at ancient sites during reconstructed ritual is exactly the open experiment proposed in 07.
