# 01 · Research Proposal

## Working title
*The Sacred Geometry of Sound: Archaeoacoustics, Religious Frequency Practice, and the Psychology of Sacred Space*

## Background

Archaeoacoustics — the study of sound at archaeological sites — emerged as a named field in the 1980s–1990s, when researchers began measuring what had previously only been anecdote: that Paleolithic painted caves, Neolithic burial chambers, Mesoamerican pyramids, and Andean temples exhibit unusual, sometimes startling acoustic behavior. In parallel, psychoacoustics (the science of how humans perceive sound) and environmental psychology (how built space affects cognition and emotion) matured into rigorous experimental disciplines.

These three fields converge on a question that religions answered in practice thousands of years ago: **what does the geometry of a space do to sound, and what does that sound do to a human being?**

## Research questions

- **RQ1 (Archaeoacoustics):** Which ancient sacred sites have *measured* anomalous or notable acoustic properties (resonance, echo effects, extreme reverberation, sound focusing), and is there evidence the builders intended those properties?
- **RQ2 (Religious practice):** How have world religions deliberately used frequency, pitch, chant, and resonant architecture, and what do their own primary texts say about sound?
- **RQ3 (Psychology of geometry):** What does controlled research show about the psychological effects of room geometry — curvature vs. angularity, ceiling height, scale, symmetry, enclosure?
- **RQ4 (Psychoacoustic mechanism):** Can the reported subjective effects at ancient sites (awe, presence, altered states) be explained by known psychoacoustic mechanisms (resonance coupling, infrasound, reverberation, expectation)?
- **RQ5 (Claims audit):** Which popular claims in this space (432 Hz, solfeggio frequencies, "psionics," cymatics-as-creation) survive source triangulation, and which collapse?

## Hypotheses

- **H1 (Intentionality — testable, contested):** Certain Neolithic chambered structures were shaped or selected partly for their acoustic behavior in the male-vocal-range (roughly 90–120 Hz resonances). *Status: suggestive evidence exists; intent is hard to prove — see 02.*
- **H2 (Mechanism — well supported):** Long reverberation, strong low-frequency resonance, and loss of visual cues jointly produce measurable shifts in mood, attention, and physiology, sufficient to explain "numinous" experience without any exotic physics.
- **H3 (Convergent design — descriptive):** Independent religious traditions converged on similar acoustic strategies (drone, chant in resonant chambers, bells/horns at ritual boundaries) because human auditory psychology is universal.
- **H4 (Null hypothesis for fringe claims):** No specific absolute frequency has intrinsic healing/spiritual efficacy beyond expectation and relaxation effects; no psionic phenomenon will survive controlled replication. *This is the position the audit tests against.*

## Key definitions (terms are used precisely in this project)

| Term | Definition used here |
|---|---|
| **Psychoacoustics** | The experimental science of auditory perception (pitch, loudness, masking, localization). Real, mainstream field. |
| **Archaeoacoustics** | Measurement and interpretation of acoustics at archaeological sites. Young but legitimate field with peer-reviewed literature. |
| **Sacred geometry** | Historically: geometric ratios (golden ratio, ad quadratum, mandala plans) used symbolically in sacred architecture. In fringe usage: the claim these ratios have intrinsic metaphysical power. This project studies the former and audits the latter. |
| **Psionics** | Umbrella term (coined in 1950s science fiction) for purported psychic abilities — telepathy, psychokinesis, remote viewing. **Not an established phenomenon**; treated in 05 as a claims-assessment subject, not a tool. |
| **Resonance** | Physical amplification of specific frequencies by a space or object. Real physics; also the most-abused word in the fringe literature. |

## Scope and limits

- **In scope:** measured site acoustics; religious sound practice with primary-text grounding; controlled psychology/neuroscience of space and sound; critical history of parapsychology; audit of frequency claims.
- **Out of scope:** endorsing any metaphysical claim; medical advice; "energy" frameworks with no measurable referent.
- **Ethical note:** several study populations (worshippers, indigenous site custodians) require respectful engagement; site measurement requires heritage-authority permission (see 07).
