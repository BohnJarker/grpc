# The Sacred Geometry of Sound

**A research project on the acoustics of ancient sacred sites, religious uses of frequency, the psychology of architectural geometry, and a critical assessment of "psionic" claims.**

---

## What this project is

Across the world, ancient builders created spaces — burial chambers, temples, cathedrals, kivas, pyramids — that do strange and powerful things to sound. Modern archaeoacoustics measures those effects with instruments; psychoacoustics and environmental psychology explain what those effects do to human perception and emotion. Around that genuine science has grown a large body of speculative and fringe claims ("healing frequencies," "psionics," "sound creates matter") that borrow the vocabulary of the science without its evidence.

This project does both jobs at once:

1. **Documents what is actually known** about the acoustics of ancient sites and the deliberate use of sound in religious practice.
2. **Audits the fringe claims** attached to this topic, using source triangulation — every claim is checked against mainstream/peer-reviewed sources and primary documents, and every source is tagged.

## Source-tagging convention (used in every document)

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[MAINSTREAM]` | Peer-reviewed research, standards bodies, established academic press |
| `[PRIMARY]` | Original documents: scripture, declassified files, first measurement reports, patents |
| `[FRINGE]` | Sources making claims that diverge from the established evidence base |

Where fringe and mainstream sources disagree, the document includes an explicit **Divergence note** explaining *why* they diverge — evidence gap, methodological failure, or genuinely open question. Fringe sources are never cited alongside mainstream ones without distinction.

## The one-paragraph honest summary

There is real, replicated science here: some ancient monuments have measurable resonances and unusual acoustic behavior; reverberant sacred architecture demonstrably shapes emotion and ritual practice; room geometry (curvature, ceiling height, scale) has measurable psychological effects; and low-frequency sound and infrasound can produce odd bodily sensations. What the evidence does **not** support: that any specific frequency (432 Hz, 528 Hz, etc.) has intrinsic healing or spiritual power, that ancient builders encoded a universal "sacred frequency," or that psionic/psychic phenomena exist as replicable effects. The most interesting defensible thesis is softer and still remarkable: **ancient builders discovered, empirically and repeatedly, that certain geometries turn a room into an instrument — and that being inside that instrument changes how people feel, which is why those geometries were used for the sacred.**
