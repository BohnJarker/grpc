# 09 · The Physics Primer: How Geometry Becomes Frequency

This is the literal "sacred geometry of sound": the mathematics by which a room's shape and size determine which frequencies it amplifies, how long sound lives inside it, and where it focuses. Everything in this file is standard acoustics `[MAINSTREAM]` — and it is the single most effective tool for auditing fringe claims, because **once you can compute what a room must do from its dimensions alone, "mysterious" resonances stop being mysterious.**

---

## 9.1 Room modes: the master equation

A hard-walled rectangular room of dimensions L×W×H supports standing waves at:

```
f(n1,n2,n3) = (c/2) · sqrt( (n1/L)² + (n2/W)² + (n3/H)² )      c ≈ 343 m/s
```

The lowest (axial) mode along a dimension L is simply **f = c/2L**.

| Longest dimension | Fundamental resonance |
|---|---|
| 1.5 m (cist grave) | ~114 Hz |
| 2 m (small chamber) | ~86 Hz |
| 3 m (Hypogeum-scale room) | ~57 Hz (with strong 2nd mode ~114 Hz) |
| 5 m (large chamber) | ~34 Hz (harmonics at 69, 103, 137 Hz…) |
| 30 m (nave width) | ~5.7 Hz (below hearing — infrasonic modal region) |

**The decisive observation for this project:** any stone chamber whose dimensions are a few meters — i.e., a chamber sized *for human bodies* — necessarily has strong low-order modes in the **60–130 Hz band**, which is also the adult male chest-voice fundamental range (~85–155 Hz; females ~165–255 Hz). The Jahn et al. 95–120 Hz cluster (02 §2.3) and the Hypogeum's ~110 Hz are therefore the *expected* physics of human-scale stone rooms. The coincidence that needs no conspiracy: **rooms built to hold people resonate where men chant.** What remains genuinely open is whether builders noticed and exploited it (H1) — which is a question about people, not physics.

## 9.2 Why stone matters: Q factor and reverberation

- **Absorption:** stone's absorption coefficient is tiny (α ≈ 0.01–0.05 across the voice band) vs. drapery/bodies/wood (0.3–0.9). Low absorption → resonances are sharp (high Q) and reverberation is long.
- **Sabine's equation:** `RT60 ≈ 0.161 · V / A` (V = volume m³, A = total absorption in m² sabins).
  - Small sealed stone chamber (V≈30 m³, A≈1): RT ≈ 5 s — enormous for a closet-sized room.
  - Gothic nave (V≈100,000 m³): RT 4–10 s, matching measured cathedral values (02 §2.9).
- Consequence: in a high-Q stone chamber, a voice matching a mode is **amplified and sustained**; the singer feels the room "answer." A skin tent, wooden hall, or open field cannot produce this experience at any frequency. Sacred acoustics is, first of all, **masonry acoustics**.

## 9.3 Feedback between voice and room (why chanting finds the mode)

A singer in a resonant chamber receives amplified feedback exactly at modal frequencies, and naturally slides pitch toward the strongest response (the same mechanism that makes people sing in the shower at the shower's mode). So no tuning knowledge is needed: **the room teaches the chant.** Over generations of ritual practice, a tradition's chanting pitch in a given chamber would converge on that chamber's mode — a fully naturalistic route to "tuned temples" that requires zero acoustical theory, only imitation. This is the project's proposed mechanism bridging H1 and H3, and it is testable (07 §7.2, B1: naive singers in a chamber-IR condition should drift toward the modal pitch).

## 9.4 Shapes and their signatures

| Geometry | Acoustic signature | Sacred-architecture instance |
|---|---|---|
| **Rectangular chamber** | Discrete modes per §9.1 | Megalithic cists, King's Chamber |
| **Cylinder/circular room** | Bessel-function modes; sound circles the wall | Kivas, round towers, tholos tombs |
| **Dome/hemisphere** | Focusing toward center/focal points; whispering-gallery modes hugging the wall (explained by Rayleigh for St Paul's, 1878 `[MAINSTREAM classic]`) | Pantheon, Hagia Sophia, mosque domes |
| **Corbelled vault** (stepped approximation of dome) | Focusing + strong vertical modes | Newgrange chamber, Treasury of Atreus, Maya vaults |
| **Long passage** (cross-section ≪ length) | Acoustic waveguide: plane-wave transmission, cutoff filtering of higher modes; open-closed tube resonances `f = c/4L, 3c/4L…` | Passage tombs, temple adyta approaches |
| **Periodic steps** | Bragg/diffraction-grating reflection: echo with frequency-dependent delay → chirp | El Castillo staircase (02 §2.7); any long stair |
| **Coupled small/large volumes** | Helmholtz-type resonance `f = (c/2π)·sqrt(S/(V·Leff))`; niches act as tuned absorbers/radiators | Hypogeum side-niches, mosque resonator cavities (03 §3.5) |
| **Ellipse/parabola** | Exact focus-to-focus sound transport | Whispering benches, "confessional" effects in apses |

The table is the project's Rosetta stone: every "anomalous" effect reported at ancient sites in 02 falls into one of these standard categories.

## 9.5 The psychoacoustic overlay (why these physics feel uncanny)

Four perceptual facts convert the physics above into numinous experience:

1. **Localization fails in reverberant/focused fields.** Human direction-finding relies on first-arrival cues (precedence effect); focused or highly reverberant fields scramble them → sound "comes from everywhere/nowhere" → attributed to non-human agency (05 §5.4).
2. **Vibrotactile threshold.** Below ~100 Hz at high SPL, sound is *felt* (chest, sinuses, eyeballs ~18 Hz) as much as heard — chant at a chamber mode delivers whole-body vibration, honestly described by participants as "energy in the body."
3. **Missing fundamental & difference tones.** Nonlinear ear behavior generates perceived tones that are not physically present — e.g., two horns a fifth apart imply a phantom octave-lower fundamental; large bells' strike-note is itself a perceptual construct.
4. **Slow modulation and beating.** Two voices/pipes slightly detuned at a strong mode produce beats at 0.5–8 Hz — the same band as trance drumming (03 §3.6) — so a droning ensemble in a resonant room self-generates its own entrainment rhythm.

## 9.6 Worked example: the Hypogeum "Oracle Room" number

Take a chamber with effective dimension ~3.1 m: second axial mode = 2·(343/2·3.1) ≈ **110 Hz** — with the first mode near 55 Hz and vertical/oblique modes filling the 60–130 Hz band. This is why the project treats "the ancients chose 110 Hz" claims (06 §3) as inverted causality: **the builders chose a room size; the room size chose the frequency.** The right research question — did they then *select, decorate, and ritualize* the best-sounding chambers? — is answerable with the site-survey design in 07 §7.1.

## 9.7 What geometry cannot do (physics constraints on fringe claims)

- Room resonance cannot store or emit "energy" beyond the sound put into it (passive linear systems; conservation).
- No room geometry can make a specific absolute frequency (432 Hz etc.) universally special: modal frequencies scale inversely with size, so *every* frequency is "sacred" to some room somewhere.
- Acoustic levitation is real physics only at extreme SPL in engineered standing-wave devices (ultrasonic levitators lifting droplets) — many orders of magnitude beyond anything a voice, horn, or chamber produces; claims of sonic levitation of megaliths `[FRINGE]` fail by roughly 10⁶× in energy terms.
- Sound in air cannot restructure DNA or matter: acoustic photon/phonon energies at audio frequencies are ~10 orders of magnitude below molecular bond energies; audible-sound bioeffects operate via hearing, vibration perception, and stress pathways — i.e., through *psychology and physiology*, which is exactly where this project looks for them (04, and the clinical-evidence review in 10).
