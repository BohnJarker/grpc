## 11.1 Chinese bianzhong bells — the best-documented ancient sound technology

The 65-bell set from the tomb of **Marquis Yi of Zeng (433 BCE)** is the strongest case anywhere of ancient *engineered* frequency control. Each bell's almond-shaped ("tile-shaped," 合瓦形) cross-section produces **two distinct musical tones** depending on strike point (center *sui* vs. side *gu*), typically a third apart, with fast decay so the tones don't smear — confirmed by finite-element modeling (*Chinese Science Bulletin* 52, 2007) and spectral analysis of the full replica set (ASA *POMA* 19, 2013). Standard monograph: von Falkenhausen, *Suspended Music* (Univ. of California Press, 1993). `[MAINSTREAM]` — deliberate, mathematically sophisticated frequency design, in a ritual context, with inscribed music theory on the bells themselves `[PRIMARY]`.

## 11.2 India — musical pillars and rock-cut halls

- **Vittala Temple, Hampi (16th c.):** the granite "musical pillars" are real tuned lithophones — nondestructive testing (*JASA* 124(2), 2008) confirmed solid granite colonettes deliberately dimensioned as flexural resonators (no hidden metal or cavities); IEEE (2012) beam-model analysis matches measured frequencies. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Nellaiappar Temple (c. 700 CE):** likely the earliest musical-pillar tradition; **no dedicated peer-reviewed measurement exists** — a documented gap.
- **Ajanta/Ellora:** famous chanting-acoustics claims remain **unmeasured**; nearest rigorous proxies are ambisonic impulse-response studies of Kanheri caves (*Sound & Vibration* 56(2), 2022) and Udaigiri caves, whose authors explicitly call for extending measurement to Ajanta/Ellora.

## 11.3 Lithophones and rock gongs — the deep-time layer

- **Vietnam:** the Ndut Lieng Krak lithophone (11 shaped schist slabs, found 1949) shows deliberate tuning tool-marks; classic publication by Condominas/Schaeffner (1952, *BEFEO*). `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **India:** Boivin (2004, *Antiquity* 78) — Kupgal Neolithic petroglyph site where percussion-marked dolerite boulders ("rock gongs") co-locate with rock art: a ritual soundscape argument in a top journal. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Africa:** rock gongs documented since Fagg (1956, *Man*) across Nigeria, Sudan, East Africa, often spatially associated with paintings. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **South Africa, 2024 (methodological model):** Rusch, Wurz & Rusch at Kurukop (Nama Karoo): impulse-response measurement found ~60% of petroglyphs made within the mapped echo zone — the quantitative echo-and-art method the field now expects. `[MAINSTREAM]`

## 11.4 Andes and Mesoamerica — beyond Chavín

- **Huánuco Pampa (Inca):** Kolar et al. (2018, *Heritage Science* 6, open access) — field survey of the ushnu platform quantifying sonic communication ranges of Inca administrative architecture with voices, Andean instruments, and test signals. `[MAINSTREAM]`
- **Moray:** "perfect amphitheater acoustics" appears only in tourism copy — **unmeasured**; flag as `[FRINGE/unsupported]`.
- **Aztec "death whistle" (2024 headline result):** Frühholz et al., *Communications Psychology* 2 — CT reconstruction of original skull whistles revealed two opposed chambers generating turbulent, scream-like sound; listening + neuroimaging experiments showed the sound is processed as aversive and "hybrid natural-artificial," engaging affective auditory circuits — consistent with ritual/sacrificial use. `[MAINSTREAM]` — a rare direct psychoacoustics-of-ritual-instrument study, exactly the B-track method of 07 applied to an artifact.

## 11.5 Japan — engineered contemplative sound

- **Suikinkutsu** (buried water-chime jars in temple gardens): mechanism established by Watanabe (2004, *Japanese Journal of Applied Physics* 43) — droplet-excited Helmholtz/cavity resonance of the inverted jar produces the koto-like ring. `[MAINSTREAM]` — a garden-scale example of geometry-as-instrument.
- **Bonshō temple bells:** modal/FEM analyses exist mostly in Japanese-language venues; the 30–60 s decay and beating "warble" (from deliberate wall-thickness asymmetry) are well characterized physically; ritual-perception studies are scarce — a gap.

## 11.6 Egypt — one real study amid heavy fringe

- **Dendera (temple of Hathor):** first genuine measurement campaign in an Egyptian temple — spherical-microphone-array directional impulse responses across the 40-room complex (IEEE I3DA 2021), tied to the scholarly soundscape program of Sibylle Emerit (IFAO) with IRCAM. `[MAINSTREAM/PRIMARY]`
- **Karnak:** an online claim of a "2017 JASA standing-wave study of the Hypostyle Hall" could **not be verified to exist** — treat as fabricated-or-garbled `[FRINGE]`. This is a live specimen of the invented-citation pattern (06 meta-pattern §4).
- **Sound levitation of blocks:** `[FRINGE]`, fails energetically by ~6 orders of magnitude (09 §9.7) and contradicts ramp/lever archaeology.

## 11.7 Eleusis and Greek mystery cults

Scholarly reconstruction is textual, not instrumental: Petridou (2018, *Body and Religion* 2.1) reconstructs the Mysteries' soundscape — the **echeion** bronze gong struck at the invocation of Kore, ritual cries, and weaponized silence. `[MAINSTREAM]` + `[PRIMARY]` (Apollodorus fragment). **No impulse-response measurement of the Telesterion has been published** — with the roofed, columned Telesterion being one of antiquity's few *enclosed* mass-initiation halls, this is arguably the field's most tantalizing open measurement.

## 11.8 Göbekli Tepe and Australia — the honest gaps

- **Göbekli Tepe (c. 9500 BCE):** the only direct "acoustic study" (SB Research Group's "14 Hz" enclosure claims) is non-reproducible self-published work — `[FRINGE]`. The methodologically sound adjacent work is McBride (2014, *World Archaeology* 46(3)), modeling reverberation and speech intelligibility in Near Eastern Neolithic communal buildings. Verdict: **world's oldest monumental site, zero rigorous acoustic data — a flagship target for Track A (07 §7.1).**
- **Aboriginal Australia:** despite the world's oldest continuous ritual sound culture (didjeridu depictions ~1,500 BP in Arnhem Land rock art; bullroarers), **no systematic peer-reviewed site-acoustics campaign exists** — only Waller's conference-level echo studies. Any future work must be custodian-partnered (07 ethics).

**Cross-cutting lesson:** the reliability gradient runs bianzhong → musical pillars/lithophones → Huánuco Pampa/death whistle → Dendera → textual Eleusis → the gaps (Göbekli, Ajanta, Australia) → fringe (SB Research Group, levitation, invented citations). The pattern from 06 holds globally: **where measurement is strong, claims get modest; where measurement is absent, claims get grandiose.**
